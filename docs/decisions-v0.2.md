# Decisions taken while building version 0.2

The decisions below were taken while version 0.2 (landmarks and swamps) was built, numbered as in the build ledger. Each says what was decided, why, and what it costs if it turns out wrong. They come from the build ledger, which lives in a git-ignored workspace, so they are kept here with the rest of the docs. The numbers are the ledger's; other docs cite them as "Ruling N".

"Task N" is a task of the v0.2 plan (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-19-v02-landmarks-swamps.md`). The tasks with a letter (8b, 10b, 17b, 17c, 20b, 21a, 21b and 22a to 22i) were follow-ups added during the build. What the in-game spikes found is in the spec's "Spike results (v0.2)" (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-landmarks-street-life-ground-design.md`). The plan and the spec are working documents of the development repository and are not part of the public source snapshot.

1. Tasks ran one at a time, even where the plan said Tasks 3, 4 and 7 could run in parallel, because parallel builders would share one Python environment, one bytecode cache and one git index. If that was wrong, it cost some wall-clock time.
2. Task 6 created `landmark_materials.yaml` with the one material its test binds (`br_lm_stucco_tan`, the game's white stucco tinted tan), and Task 8 added the rest. If that was wrong, Task 8 only has to edit the entry.
3. `materials.resolve` named the texture maps by the stock convention `<id>_<suffix>.png`, and left Task 8's spike to decide whether shipped `.dds` files load under those names. The cost was one small change in Task 8 (Ruling 14 settled it).
4. Steps that launch the game wait while Noah is playing on GeForce NOW, so the game-free tasks (1 to 7, 12, 14 and 13) ran first, with the texture downloads held until he agreed. That reorders the plan and costs nothing if wrong.
5. The plan's "181-289 m" for the Huey P. Long Bridge became "181 m or more", because its decks run from y 8,373 to 8,532 m and the box edge is at 8,192 m. It is a wording fix in the docs only.
6. Commits by the Sonnet builders end with "Claude Sonnet 5.5", their own session's attribution, instead of the plan's "Claude Opus 5.5". It was accepted as accurate and the commits were not rewritten, so nothing is lost.
7. Task 8's review found its PNG-versus-DDS spike confounded (both faces shared a file stem, so the game might have loaded the `.dds` beside the `.png`). The finding was accepted as real but not blocking, since the decision to ship only the `.dds` does not depend on the PNG face and Task 8b removed the split. It costs nothing.
8. Tower heights are measured above the ground (the ground offset plus the model height), and the finial above the model height, because published heights are heights above grade. It costs nothing.
9. After Task 10's fix the bayou road cut checks each ribbon node's cross-section, but it still left ribbon over a drivable corridor in 7 places on the full map (41.9 m2 in all) and 2 on the LSU map (2.2 m2). That was accepted because the ribbons sit at channel level, below the road surface, so the terrain should hide them; if water shows on a road, tightening the cut is a small follow-up (Task 10b then took the overlap to 0).
10. The full-map scale test passed its memory gate (4.69 GB against 7 GB), and the 300 Rivers cost only 0.6 s of the load, so the plan's fallback of fewer and coarser bayous would not help and was not applied. The 156.4 s cold load, over the 150 s gate but under the 180 s budget, was to be measured again on a quiet machine before any change; the cost if wrong is that re-measure and, if still over, a look at the 94.5 s `TSStatic` stage.
11. The bayous' width smoothing is a 5-node moving average of the 5-node maximum, not a plain average, because a plain average doubles the share of ribbon edges hanging more than 0.2 m above the ground (3.98 % to 7.65 %). It costs about 24 % more water, mostly under rising ground, and 165 more trees cleared; reverting is one line and six tests.
12. Task 17's review found that `orthogonalize` grew the OpenStreetMap outlines to cover their rectangles (Prescott by 198 m2). That is a real defect, so the styled buildings now skip the rectangle cover and their walls stay within 1 m of the OSM outline (Task 17b).
13. BeamNG.drive updated itself to 0.40.0.0 on 2026-10-19, and GeForce NOW runs the current game, so version 0.2 targets 0.40: the spike findings are checked again on it, the compiled meshes and imposters are collected again, and the constraint "0.39.4" reads "the current game (0.40.0)". The cost if wrong is those re-checks.
14. Material texture references follow BeamNG 0.40's `NOTE_FOR_MODDERS.md`: they keep the `.png` name and only the `.dds` ships beside it, which reverses Task 8b's direct `.dds` reference. It was confirmed in game on 0.40, where the clock dial renders with only the DDS shipped.
15. `br_lm_roof_tile` went back to the game's own Italian roof tiles, because the CC0 RoofingTiles012A set at 1 m UVs draws tiles about 7.7 cm across. It costs nothing.
16. Landmarks get a ground outline from their collision meshes, not just the OpenStreetMap footprint, for the road check and the clear zones, so a part that overhangs its footprint (the PMAC's podium) counts. The first version took a convex hull of each part, which overreached concave footprints (Tiger Stadium's bowl by 16 m), and Task 17c replaced it.
17. The ground outline closes the collision meshes' walls before it takes their union: the meshes are floorless shells, so the literal triangle union lost the PMAC's overhang and shrank Tiger Stadium to its field. Tiger Stadium's outline is then 50,315 m2, equal to its footprint; if wrong, a future free-standing perimeter wall would clear its whole interior.
18. The plan's rule of no piers in the Mississippi polygon left about 190 m of I-10 deck east of the truss without piers, which is a real defect, so ordinary piers are allowed in the river polygon except within the truss footprint and the reach of its end piers (Task 21a). It costs nothing.
19. The plan's rule that the `dr_a60` level of detail holds piers only leaves tiles that carry only barriers with one visible level of detail. That was accepted because a barrier is too thin to read past about 60 px and the rule targets landmark and tree models; it costs nothing.
20. Moss overlays have no collision mesh, as the plan says, because moss must not collide and the oak it hangs on keeps its own collision. It costs nothing.
21. `NARROW_OUT` is 0.25 m, which meets the 3.4 m between barrier bases that a one-lane ramp needs and leaves the whole carriageway drivable, at the price of 0.55 m of each barrier base hanging past the slab's edge; `NARROW_DECK` stays 7.0 m, so 155 of the 252 decks (65 of them two-lane 6.5 m street bridges) get barriers just outside the carriageway. The cost if wrong is a look at the underside of those decks in game.
22. The moss texture's mip chain keeps its coverage by a closed-loop measure on the decoded blocks, the alpha reference range is 1 to 255 (0 would pass every texel), and the alpha-test reference values live in a separate `ALPHA_REF` table beside the generated textures. If that was wrong, it costs a second table to keep in step, which a test guards (documented in `textures.md`).
23. The licence plate's background is plain white (`#ffffff`, the stock default's own colour) instead of the stock default image, because the only 30-15 image in the game's default plate folder is a state's plate, and the map carries no real state graphics. If wrong, one constant changes.
24. The README and the mod-page text must say what the dressing stage builds, not "barriers on the 252 decks, and piers under them": the piers stand under 116 of the decks, and two 6 m ramp stubs had no barrier at all. Public text should be true, and fixing it costs nothing. Task 22c later gave the two stubs a short piece beside their joint, so the text now says barriers on all 252 decks with the two stubs short (Task 22e).
25. The Spanish moss card is drawn at a base colour factor of (0.65, 0.65, 0.5), between the two factors that read as moss in game (0.75 was silvery, 0.55 grey-green) and warm to offset the blue 0.40 sky. If it looks wrong, it is one material value.
26. All the verified findings of the final whole-branch review, and its minor ones, are fixed in one wave (Tasks 22a to 22h) before the release, because version 0.1 is not approved yet and time is not the constraint. It costs one more full build and in-game load.
27. Barrier gaps at straight, same-level deck joints are closed (Task 22c), overriding the plan's blanket end trim and corridor cut at deck ends there only, because a driver expects a continuous parapet on I-10. The price is an exemption in the validator near those joints, where a barrier now runs into the next deck's corridor.
28. Landmarks keep 0.5 m (`LANE_CLEAR`) off the drivable corridors of the roads beside them by cutting their footprint, rather than moving the OpenStreetMap road, because that is general and guards future landmarks. It costs Tiger Stadium's east side stepping in by up to 1.9 m over about 67 m, which was checked again in game.
29. The licence plate folder stays in the zip, which removes 0.40's missing-plate warning, and the price is that a fresh install may be typed "unknown" by the mod manager (see the guidelines table in [repo-submission.md](repo-submission.md)). If that matters, dropping the folder from `zip_mod` brings the warning back instead.
30. The Capitol is exempt from the lane cut for only two service drives (w97757425 and w824026179), which end on the covered passage under its wings, instead of the brief's rule to keep a cut under 2 %: the literal cut would push the Capitol's model past its triangle budget (381 against 300) for 3.5 m2 of slivers. The cost is that those two corridors go unchecked at the Capitol.
31. The swamp-pool rule that dropped any box overlapping another water body's box was too strict, which was an error in the brief: it dropped batture ground above the other water's level where nothing clashed, leaving the pools over 39 % of the low wetland cells. A visible-water rule replaced it (Task 22g): the pool level is computed over the cells the pool can draw, boxes that draw nothing are dropped, and the check runs on centimetre-rounded values; after Task 22h (decision 34) the full map's 7 pools in 218 blocks cover 61 % of the low wetland cells (8,797 of 14,380), and at worst pools at different levels meet within 2 cells.
32. The lane cut could silently remove a road that runs through a landmark from the validator's view, so `resolve` records what the cut took and the validator reports a cut over 2 % of the footprint, or one that splits it or leaves a hole (Task 22f). It costs nothing.
33. The validator keeps its original test for a road through a landmark, a centreline across the footprint shrunk 0.5 m, and runs it on the footprint as it was before the lane cut. A dead-end road that runs deep into a landmark makes only a notch in the cut footprint (a 6.5 m road 70 m into a 200 m square cuts 1.4 %), which the check of the cut's share lets pass, and the cut footprint no longer meets the road's centreline. So `resolve` records those roads in `lane_cut.through` and the validator reports each as passing through the landmark (Task 22h); the ways a landmark exempts are not recorded. It costs nothing: Tiger Stadium and the Capitol stay clean on the full map.
34. The check that drops a swamp pool whose level is under the 5th percentile of its ground (`SHOW_PCT`) takes all the cells of the swamp's polygon again, not only the cells the pool can draw as Task 22g had it, because a road caps the level of the largest batture swamp (w1462559887) at 6.68 m, under the 5th percentile of its drawable cells (6.85 m), and the check dropped the whole pool. The blocks that draw none of the swamp's water are dropped later anyway, so the check only has to stop a pool that would show no water at all, and the level still comes from the drawable cells (`POOL_PCT`). On the full map that gives 7 pools in 218 blocks covering 8,797 of the 14,380 low wetland cells (61 %), with at most 2 cells per block over visible lower water (Task 22h). If wrong, a pool that draws almost nothing stays, at the price of its blocks.
35. Version 0.2 ships with two small pool flaws written up in the known issues instead of another round of pool rules: up to 2 cells (4 m grid) per swamp-pool block may still show pool water over the river's or a lake's edge (49 cells, about 780 m2 on the full map, mostly on the Mississippi's bank), and in places a block's side ends above lower ground (about 1.2 km of edges). Neither shows in the in-game swamp views, and version 0.3 refines the pools. If wrong, a player who looks closely along the batture sees small raised patches of water or a water edge in the air.
